HOW IT WORKS
From creator discovery to reviewed outreach
Outreach V4 combines three services: EasyKOL provides creator data, an OpenAI language model supports analysis and writing, and Gmail handles email communication. The application connects these services into one workflow.





1
EasyKOL supplies creator information


Users enter a creator description and filters such as region or follower range. The Python backend requests matching profiles and available business contact information from EasyKOL, then saves the results as a contact list.


This gives the AI actual profile facts to work with instead of asking it to invent creators or email addresses; third-party data may still be incomplete or outdated.







2
OpenAI connects creator facts with brand goals


Users provide a brand description and campaign goals, then select a creator. The application combines that brief with available profile facts and optional video metadata, and sends the relevant information through the Responses API for two tasks.


  • Creator fit analysis: explain why the creator may or may not suit the brand, suggest a collaboration angle, and identify information still missing.
  • Personalized writing: generate three opening options with reasons and source links, so users can choose an option and edit the email before sending.


The model uses a transformer architecture, with attention helping connect relevant parts of the input. Fluent writing can still contain mistaken interpretations, so AI output remains a draft for human review.







3
Gmail sends approved emails and retrieves replies


Google OAuth lets users authorize their own mailbox through Google without entering an app-specific email password in this connection flow. After the user reviews and confirms the draft, the application can send it through the Gmail API.


With optional reply access and reply tracking enabled, the application retrieves matching replies and updates the outreach record. Sending and reply matching are handled by application logic; the language model helps with analysis and writing.





DEPLOYMENT ARCHITECTURE
Where the application runs




Local workspace
The interface opens in a browser, while a Python backend runs on each teammate’s computer and manages local contacts, drafts, settings, and records.


Shared AI gateway on Render
AI requests pass through a shared gateway that keeps the OpenAI API key on the server and applies per-member request limits, so teammates do not need individual AI API keys.

 RISKS & WHERE IT BREAKS 
What a machine can check, we block in code. What needs judgment, we hand to a person.
 
In real use, a lot of unexpected things happen, so we tested our tool with tricky inputs on purpose. Sample campaign: promote CeraVe Skin Renewing Vitamin C Serum to US TikTok creators who share skincare content. (Class demo, not affiliated with CeraVe.)
 
 
 
Checked in code
  • Quotes the AI made up
  • Answers in the wrong format
  • Creators with no profile data
  • AI service errors
  • Emails nobody confirmed
 
Needs a person
  • Reading tone, like a complaint
  • Deciding what is fair to use, like race or health
  • Judging whether a pitch makes sense
 
 
 
1
What works: unrelated creators get flagged
To test whether the AI would hallucinate a reason for creators who don't fit at all, we matched US gaming streamers against a skincare brief.
Answers Q3 · Q1
 
 
Before choosing a brand
One streamer is a “Strong fit”, because the AI is matching him to the search for gaming streamers, not to the product.
 
After choosing CeraVe
All five come back as Limited fit and Low priority. The tool moves them down instead of deleting them, so a person makes the final call.
 
 
A harder case: US makeup artists came back as “Potential fit”, because most of them also post skincare. The AI can tell a close match from an unrelated one, and keeping them in leaves room for creative angles, like the serum as skin prep before makeup.
 
 
 
2
Where it goes wrong: a bad recommendation
It reads the text correctly, then makes the wrong call.
Answers Q1 · Q3
 
 
The creator is an esthetician with rosacea and sensitive skin. Her caption says a vitamin C serum stung, broke her out, and she will never use one again. The AI turned that complaint into a pitch for another vitamin C serum. The quote was real, so our check passed it. The judgment was wrong. Other drafts also put her skin condition into a business email.
  
 
 
For now, a person catches it
  • The AI only suggests three openings; nothing goes into the email until the user clicks “Use this opening”
  • Every option links to its Evidence source, so you can see it used her complaint
  • Nothing is sent until someone checks the confirmation box
  • This still depends on a careful person. We need to improve it.
  
 
 
 
3

The AI used race as evidence, and the user can't see it.
Answers Q1 · Q4
 
 
To explain a match, the AI quotes the creator's profile tags. Those tags come from our data provider and include race. Our guardrail only checks that each quote is real, and these are, so they pass. The page shows just “Source: Profile topics”.

STATUS & NEXT STEPS
Working Now
Our core APIs are connected, and we have completed several rounds of testing with our own test accounts. The main workflow is generally functional.
Creator search and contact import
Connected to EasyKOL. Users can search for creators through EasyKOL or import and add their own contacts.
Creator and brand matching analysis
Users can review a creator’s profile and generate an AI analysis of how well they fit the brand.
Personalized email options and editable email preview
The tool generates three opening options based on the creator and brand information. Users edit the message, preview it, and decide whether to send it.
Gmail connection and optional reply tracking
Gmail connects through Google sign-in. Reply tracking is opt-in.
Challenges We Hit and Next step
1
Creator fit and profile reliability, and bias
The matching analysis uses the information available in the system. It does not mean that the AI has watched the creator’s videos or verified that the account is authentic. We also found that a real quote can still support a biased recommendation. In one test, the AI used provider-added profile labels such as “Black” and “Asian” as evidence for a match.
Next:
  • Show what each analysis is based on and what is missing.
  • Judge fit and data reliability separately, and show each contact’s data source and retrieval time.
  • Let users correct errors and pick a reason, so we can see what kinds of mistakes happen.
  • Test whether filtering sensitive labels, such as race or health-related information, before AI analysis can reduce this risk. We will also show users the exact words behind each match reason.
2
Competitive positioning and business value
We aim to help brands reach smaller or newer creators outside TikTok One, but we haven’t compared our strengths and weaknesses with existing tools, or confirmed these users exist.
Next:
We will compare Outreach V4 with TikTok One, creator outreach platforms such as Modash and GRIN, and the manual process. We will examine criteria that matter to small brands, including creator data, outreach workflow, team collaboration, time, and cost. We will also gather feedback through a few interviews with potential users.
3
Emails from a new sender sometimes land in spam.
Some of our test emails landed in spam. So far, we have only tested between our own mailboxes, so we do not yet know how often this happens or what causes it.
Next:
Test with consenting volunteers across Gmail, Outlook and school inboxes. Change one factor at a time (email content, send frequency, sender account status) and record where emails land, to see which factor matters most. Then improve and retest.
